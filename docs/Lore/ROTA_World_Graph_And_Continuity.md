# ROTA — World Graph and Continuity

## Purpose
This document acts as the continuity backbone for all lore generation. It ensures every new faction, region, quest, raid, boss, relic, and item line can connect back to a clear source, anchor point, and progression path.

The goal is not simply “more content.” The goal is a world that feels authored, layered, and explicitly connected.

---

## I. The World Spine

### 1. Origin State
- Aldenmor is the world
- The Sundermarch is the game region
- The Age of Dawn is the forgotten source
- The Age of Quiet is the living present

### 2. Primary Tension
- The Ancients slept beneath the world
- The Old Guard held the line during the Sundering
- Malachar is attempting to force a new awakening through extraction and ritual violence
- The player is drawn into the consequence of that awakening by the act of combat itself

### 3. Story Engine
Every major content thread should point back to one of these pressures:
- rival factions contesting the frontier
- old-guard ruins continuing to function
- ancient resonance waking where the world is most disturbed
- a local tragedy becoming a larger world event

---

## II. The Continuity Rule

Every piece of content must have these five anchors:

1. Place — where it exists in the world
2. Origin — what historical or mythic event caused it to matter
3. Function — why it matters in gameplay or politics
4. Consequence — what damage or change it leaves behind
5. Residue — what artifact, rumor, ritual, or scar remains after it is over

If an idea lacks any of these, it is not yet strong enough to be included in the canon.

---

## III. End-to-Start Structure

### The start of a quest should always imply a larger world problem
A player should never feel like they are entering a random location. They should feel like they are walking into a fracture in the world that has a cause and a wider consequence.

### The end of a raid should always echo the start of a larger arc
A raid ends with a wound, not a dead end. It should leave behind:
- a relic
- a rumor
- a faction consequence
- a zone that now feels different
- a clue toward the larger threat beneath the region

### The end of a region arc should feed the next pressure point
Every region should not be isolated. It should point toward:
- a new frontier
- a stronger Ancient resonance
- a faction war
- a breach in old-guard security
- a deeper, more dangerous site beneath the current one

---

## IV. Genre Calibration

ROTA should feel like DOTD in the following ways:
- desperation in the frontier
- oppressive ancient weight
- brutal combat in ruined ruin-spaces
- ritualized danger and relic obsession
- a sense that the world is waking and not all of it is sane

ROTA should differ from DOTD in these ways:
- more political and military fragmentation
- stronger ideological conflict between factions
- a more grounded old-world frontier identity
- more emphasis on the Old Guard's practical war and failed defense
- more focus on region-specific ruin logic rather than a single pure “dungeon fantasy” mode

The world should feel like a living frontier under pressure, not a corridor of generic boss content.

---

## V. Faction and World Graph Logic

### Faction graph
Every faction should have:
- a place in the world
- a political stake
- a relationship to the Ancients or the Old Guard
- a method of extracting or controlling power
- a rival or enemy lineage

### Example relationship map
- Iron Weir = practical frontier defense and old-war continuity
- Dawnward Choir = spiritual resonance and active interpretation of the Ancients
- Dusk Ledger = relic economics and debt-driven power
- Malachar's Vanguard = hostile extraction and forced Awakening
- Threnody Houses = memory and archive continuity
- Hollow March = survivor frontier populations in the broken zones

Each of these should have a cross-link to at least one other faction and at least one region.

---

## VI. Region Logic

### Region should be more than a map slice
A region must answer:
- What happened here?
- Why does it still matter?
- Who owns the story of it now?
- What is buried beneath it?
- Why does the player keep returning?

### Strong region identity examples
- Ironspine Front = old war, gate lines, failed defense, mountain resonance
- Drowned Coast = sea-buried civilization, drowned memory, tidal cultism
- Ruined Frontier = constant warfare, scavenging, frontier collapse, survivors in the wake of the Old Guard

---

## VII. Quest, Raid, Boss, Loot Continuity Template

Every quest should be able to map to:
- a regional source
- a raid cluster
- a boss or boss family
- a relic or gear family
- a faction stake

### Template
Quest: “A place that should have been quiet but has become active”
Raid: “The active consequence of that disturbance”
Boss: “The form the disturbance took”
Loot: “The residue of what the event left behind”
Faction: “Who is using or exploiting the wound”

This is the backbone of all content writing.

---

## VIII. Quality Standard for Lore Writing

The writing must feel intentional and disciplined.

### High-quality lore should do the following:
- establish the place with a concrete image
- explain why it matters without overexplaining
- tie emotional weight to the setting
- provide a believable survival logic for the people in it
- let mystery remain, but never become vague filler

### Avoid these common failures
- generic fantasy names with no world logic
- disconnected zone names with no locale continuity
- overused phrasing that reads like placeholder text
- lore that explains everything and kills the mystery
- world events with no consequence or afterimage

---

## IX. Worldbuilding Production Standard

For every new content pass, require the following before accepting it:

1. Source region identified
2. Historical cause named
3. Active threat identified
4. Faction interest named
5. Raid or boss extension mapped
6. Relic or item residue attached
7. Future consequence noted

If any of these are missing, the content is not complete.

---

## X. World Graph Summary

The world is a network of pressure points:
- old war scars
- active frontline ruins
- drowned lost infrastructure
- faction conflict
- ancient resonance being forced upward
- players crossing each fracture and taking part in the cost

Every new artifact should strengthen that graph.

The world should never feel like a list of disconnected zones. It should feel like a system of wounds that are being reopened and reinterpreted by the people who live in them.

---

## XI. Future Content Priorities

1. A full Old Guard survivor network and hidden archive structure
2. A second major regional arc beyond the Ironspine and Drowned Coast
3. A faction war arc connecting the Weir, the Choir, and Malachar's Vanguard
4. A deeper explanation of how the Sundering affected the world physically, not only mythically
5. Additional raid families built from the same existing regions rather than new disconnected maps
6. A more detailed relic lineage tied to actual historical sites and old-guard command structures

---

## XII. Final Principle
ROTA must always feel authored.

Not random. Not generic. Not a pile of title cards.

It should feel like a world where every place has a memory, every faction has a logic, every raid is a consequence, and every relic carries the scar of a choice that the world still lives with.
