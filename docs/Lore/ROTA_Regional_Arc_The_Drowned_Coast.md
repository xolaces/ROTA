# ROTA — Regional Arc: The Drowned Coast

## Overview
The Drowned Coast is not a shore. It is a wound in the map.

The old southern coast fell into the sea during the Sundering when the Ancients withdrew and the geological resonance of the world failed in a single catastrophic motion. The sea advanced inland, swallowing whole roads, harbor cities, and the old trade corridors that once fed the interior. In the Age of Quiet, what remains is not simply a haunted coast but a place where the world keeps trying to remember it was once dry.

This is a region for naval warfare, ruin scavenging, relic hunting, and the psychic weight of a place that remembers a civilization no one alive can name.

---

## I. The Coast as a Place

### The Broken Coastline
The Drowned Coast is a ring of ruined ports, flood-collapsed keeps, and waterlogged roads where foundations rise from black water like bones. The wind carries salt and old iron. The air smells of rot, seepage, and something metallic that is not rain.

The region is alive with:
- flooded keep complexes
- submerged shrines
- storm-racked watchtowers
- coral-like growths around ancient sigil-stones
- relic-divers stripping wreckage for old-guard material

### The Human Settlement Pattern
Most communities on the Drowned Coast live on stilts, floating barges, or raised causeways. The old sea is not a border; it is a landlord. Every settlement pays with labor, taxes, or sacrifices to keep its foundations stable.

The most resilient communities survive by trading in four things:
1. old-guard salvage
2. sea-born monsters and their trophies
3. awakening residue from flooded ruins
4. protected shipping lanes through the outskirts

---

## II. Political and Social Tension

### The Broken Merchant Cities
The coast is full of municipal states that have survived by turning ruin into infrastructure. They are not romantic. They are mercantile, brutal, and obsessed with controlling access to collapsed roads, sunken keeps, and the old maritime channels.

These city-states prefer a simple logic:
- keep the sea from swallowing the ports
- keep rival houses from controlling the salvage routes
- keep the religious movements from turning the dead waters into a cult destination

### The Tidal Cults
Some religious groups believe the sea took the old coast because it was meant to be *returned to the deep*. They gather at drowned shrines and speak of the returning tide as a judgment rather than an event. They are not loved by the merchants and not entirely dismissed by the scholars.

### The Ruin Hounds
The Drowned Coast has also spawned a class of scavengers and wreck-runners who pursue deeper and more dangerous relic sites beneath the flooded ruins. They are not hero archetypes. They are people who know the sea is never empty and that every wreck has teeth.

## III. Factions of the Drowned Coast

### 1. The Sable Harrow
A naval confederation of broken lords, sea captains, and old-guard salvage brokers. They control the most valuable shipping lanes and constantly raid each other's anchors for salvage rights.

- Leader: Adra Vale
- Symbol: black sail crossed by a rusted sigil-band
- Doctrine: “The sea keeps only what the harbor can defend.”
- Rivalry: hates the Tide Choir and resents the Dusk Ledger's speculative salvage contracts

### 2. The Tide Choir
A religious-political movement that practices ritual listening for the ancient pulse beneath the sea. They insist the drowning was not an accident but a deliberate loosening of the world. They consider the sea an oracle and the drowned cities a library.

- Leader: High Reader Nhal Vey
- Symbol: a circle of drowned stones and a single eye
- Doctrine: “The deep remembers the first truth.”
- Rivalry: the Sable Harrow sees them as destabilizers; the Dawnward Choir sees them as dangerous and possibly sincere

### 3. The Iron Dredgers
A working caste that dives and dredges flooded keeps for black iron, null-sigil fragments, and buried old-guard engines. They are paid in coin, protection, and the right to keep what they recover only if the owners allow it.

- Leader: Brann Quillmark
- Symbol: a hooked anchor crossed by a hammer
- Doctrine: “Every ruin gives back what it can afford.”
- Rivalry: despised by merchants for creating market chaos; feared by all because they often find things nobody wants to claim

### 4. The Hush Guild
A deep-secretive broker order that sells information, false maps, and quiet contracts. They thrive on missing people, erased records, and this region's half-submerged routes.

- Leader: Maer Tallow
- Symbol: a black lantern behind a sealed veil
- Doctrine: “A map is only honest when the owner is dead.”
- Rivalry: hated by the Dusk Ledger for ruining transactions and by every faction that values ordinary truth

## IV. Commanders and Threat Figures

### Adra Vale
The Sable Harrow's admiral. She believes the coast is no longer a frontier but a claim. She holds ports with a cold, almost ritual discipline and has led more than one coastal siege through floodwater rather than open battle.

### High Reader Nhal Vey
A woman who can trigger a silent ritual and make the sea surface still for a heartbeat. She keeps a staff of dead coral and claims it was grown beneath the drowned city of Serev.

### Brann Quillmark
A dredger-prince with scarred hands, half-melted earplates, and impossible patience. He collects broken machine parts the way other men collect trophies.

### Maer Tallow
The sort of broker who can arrange a death or a pardon with the same expression. They say he never keeps a ledger in the same city twice.

### Captain Sorel Dain
A privateer who keeps one Old Guard sigil on his ship and one on his wrist. He is famed for surviving raids where the target ship simply sank before he fired a shot.

### Reth Khel
A deep-ruin warden whose people hunt unstable old-guard sites before they become living hazards. He knows the difference between relic and trap and pays for his certainty with blood.

### Ysrin Oth
A tide-obsessed scholar whose work is half theological and half engineering. He believes the drowned cities are physically resonant with the Ancient Withdrawal and may be the key to understanding the Sundering.

## V. Major Raids and Encounter Clusters

### 1. The Black Bell of Vhal
A bell tower that emerged from the flood and now rings without being touched. The tower calls the dead or the hungry, depending on the storm.

### 2. Serev Underwater Vault
A drowned archive complex built under ancient breakwater walls. Every room is half salt and half memory. The air carries the sensation of old authority.

### 3. The Grinning Jetty
A felled portside pier used by cult smugglers and wreck-runners. It is haunted by those who drowned trying to keep the coast secure.

### 4. The Floodglass Sanctum
A ruin in which old-guard sigils have begun to project mirrored visions of drowned streets, impossible tides, and people who are not yet alive.

### 5. The Anchor of the Tenth Licence
A massive wrecked warship suspended in shallow harbor water. It is said to still carry a binding engine and a crew of dead officers who never accepted the sea as their final master.

### 6. The Hollow Harbor
A once-busy port now collapsed into a tidal sink. It is a living maze among shattered warehouses, black-water channels, and drowned market towers.

### 7. Kharrow's Step
A broken old-guard causeway that rises into the sky and then drops below the waterline in intervals. It is used for ritual crossing, smuggling, and ambush.

### 8. The Quiet Grotto
A submerged chamber beneath a dead shrine where the sea surface never ripples, even in storm. Those who pass through it hear voices from the drowned city beneath the waterline.

## VI. Quest Arcs and Regional Story Hooks

### The Third Harbor
The player is sent to investigate a merchant fleet that vanished after hearing a bell from the sea. The route leads into a war between old-guard salvage crews and a cult of tidal heralds.

### The Price of the Causeway
A local lord wants a route reopened to the inland trade roads. The work requires clearing a chain of ancient floodworks while trying not to wake what is sleeping beneath them.

### The Glass Beneath the Tide
A scholar asks for help retrieving a flooded relic vault before the Tide Choir can claim it. The relics are real, but the vault contains a memory-pattern that is still active.

### The Last Ship of Aven
A wrecked ship is believed to contain a sealed old-guard engine and a chain of records tying it to the Sundering itself. Everyone wants it. Nobody can agree who should be allowed to interpret it.

### The Choir at the Broken Gate
The Drowned Coast's ceremonial shrines are opening in sequence. The Old Guard's drowned sites are beginning to resonate in a specific pattern, and the pattern matches a ritual tied to Awakening Essence.

- Deep Reach Sigil
- Broken Tide Reliquary
- Silted Halo

These items are not purely holy. They are resonant with drowned memory and carry undercurrents of old rituals.

### The Tenth Licence Relic
A lost war relic of the old coast—supposedly sealed inside the wreck of a once-mighty cruiser. It is rumored to be the surviving doctrine of how the old sea was defended in the Age of Dawn.

## VIII. The Drowned Coast in the Larger World

The Drowned Coast is the place where the world remembers the Sundering as a physical event. Not a myth. Not a story. A literal retreat. A piece of the map no longer there.

It is also the place where the player can feel the world is about to change again.

The coast keeps producing signs of old-guard resonance, submerged relics, and tide-born manifestations. The deeper the player pushes, the more it becomes obvious that the old world did not simply drown. It was made to yield.

That makes the Drowned Coast not only a content region but a thematic one: a place where the past did not vanish, it sank and waits under the waterline.

## IX. Expansion Opportunities

1. Add a full city-state rivalry among three flood ports.
2. Build a major naval raid around a flooded old-guard capital.
3. Create a relic-seeking chapter arc that forces the player to choose between preserving or monetizing the past.
4. Introduce a tide-born boss family that only appears during storms or high tide.
5. Write a hidden cult narrative about the deep sea being an ancient archive of human memory.
6. Make a regional prestige system around salvage rights, port fealty, and drowned-ruin access.
7. Add a guild or faction whose identity is tied entirely to the sea and shipping routes.
8. Tie the Drowned Coast into Malachar's bigger architectural program of Awakening extraction.
9. Expand the concept of the Drowned Coast into a chapter where the player can feel the world is subtly changing underfoot.
10. Introduce a final reveal: not all drowned ruins are natural sinkings; some were intentionally submerged to protect something.

---

## Tone to Keep
The Drowned Coast should not feel beautiful. It should feel inevitable.

It should have the same emotional weight as the ruins of the Old Guard, but in a different register: salt, flood, absence, corrosion, and all the terrible patience of a sea that does not care who it takes next.
