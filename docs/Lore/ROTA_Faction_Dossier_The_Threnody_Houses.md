# ROTA — Faction Dossier: The Threnody Houses

## Overview
The Threnody Houses do not believe they are a faction in the ordinary sense. They believe they are the custodians of memory that the world has tried to bury.

They trace their lineage to the last surviving scribes, archivists, ritualists, and line-keepers of the Age of Dawn. Their power is not in standing armies. Their power is in what they know, what they preserve, and what they consider the world is not yet ready to remember.

Their houses are spread across ruined archive halls, intelligence vaults, and secret line-keeper chapels hidden in the old warning zones. They are admired, feared, and rarely trusted.

---

## I. The Houses and their Philosophies

### House Sable Vein
The house of grave-scribes and memory mourners. They preserve the dead names and record the consequences of old wars.

### House Cinder Ledger
The house of ritual accounting and old-sigil mathematics. They believe every act of history leaves a residue and that residue can be mapped.

### House Veyr
A line of old-guard descent-holders who maintain continuity claims over certain defences, ruins, and authority tokens. They are the most politically dangerous because they can no longer be dismissed as mere scholars.

### House Rookglass
The house of mirror-ritual and threshold interpretation. Their members are feared because they speak as though they can hear the world making decisions in the dark.

---

## II. Core Belief
The Threnody Houses hold one simple conviction:

Memory is a weapon if it remembers the right thing.

They do not seek the immortality of kings. They seek continuity. They believe the world cannot survive another round of forgetting. Their doctrine is not anti-progress. It is anti-erasure.

This makes them dangerous because they are willing to guard the past even when the past is already active, dangerous, and unfinished.

---

## III. Rituals and Authority
The Houses use old-guard techniques that were never meant to be public. They preserve:
- burial records in sealed chambers
- forbidden sigil mathematics
- memory-bound relics
- witness texts written in impossible scripts
- threshold rituals that allow an object to speak its original purpose

Their authority depends on being the only group willing to treat old memory as a system rather than superstition.

---

## IV. Commanders and Keepers

### Lady Ashrel Vein
The matron of the Threnody Houses. She acts as a kind of living archive, carrying contradictions without rupturing. She is harder to read than most leaders because her calm is not serenity; it is mastery over silence.

### Brother Thal Ren
A funerary ritualist who can map the emotional residue of a dead battlefield and turn it into a practical warning. He is deeply respected by the Houses and deeply feared by everyone else.

### Serev Olt
A line-keeper and military archivist whose work is partly historical and partly tactical. He can tell which old fortifications are structurally unstable and which ones are only spiritually dangerous.

### Maella Kharrow
A relic interpreter whose ability to decode old-guard texts has made her a target for both the Dusk Ledger and Malachar's agents.

### Pyl Arveth
A keeper of witness relics, a person who can hold a relic's memory in a contained emotional state for hours without losing themselves.

---

## V. Key Conflicts

### With the Dusk Ledger
The Ledger treats relics as investment. The Threnody Houses treat them as memory. This is not a small difference. It determines who is allowed to touch the past and how.

### With the Dawnward Choir
The Choir treats resonance as something to answer. The Houses treat resonance as something to record and never let become a command. They are not enemies exactly, but they are profoundly incompatible.

### With Malachar's Vanguard
The Vanguard sees old knowledge as a resource. The Houses see it as obligation. Malachar's people are simply the most dangerous audience for that belief.

---

## VI. Why They Matter
The Houses are the world’s memory-bearers. Without them, the player would live in a world that had forgotten what the old war was, and what the failure to hold the line actually cost.

They are also a necessary counterweight to the more active, violent factions. They do not want to awaken the Ancients. They want to preserve the real arc of what happened to the world and prevent it from being reinterpreted into a conquest narrative.

---

## VII. Game Use Cases
- archive exploration and tomb-delving
- relic interpretation quests
- memory-lost boss mechanics
- secret old-guard vault dives
- faction diplomacy and dangerous negotiation arcs
- lore-based unlocks tied to ruined history

---

## VIII. Expansion Hooks
The Houses are ideal for a deeper old-world mystery arc: a hidden archive that is not merely historical, but still active enough to influence the present.

That makes them one of the clearest paths toward a larger narrative in which the world is not just being attacked from the outside, but being rewritten by the memory of what came before.
