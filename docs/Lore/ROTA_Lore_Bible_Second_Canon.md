# ROTA — Lore Bible: Second Canon
### The Bestiary & The Armory
*Companion to First Canon. Compiled 2026-06-11.*
*Status: Newly established canon — not yet in the codebase.*
*This document extends First Canon; it supersedes nothing in it.*
*All proper nouns from prior canon are preserved exactly. New proper nouns are listed in §IX.*

---

## 0. WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS

First Canon answered *what the world is* — Aldenmor, the Sundermarch, the two Ages, the Sundering, the Ancients, the Old Guard, Kronarch's seal.

It left two things thin:

1. **The Ancients are not the only things in the dark.** A world this old is full of horrors that have nothing to do with the four primal forces. The Old Guard did not spend an entire Age contemplating Wrath — they spent it *killing things*. Most of what they killed were not Ancients.
2. **"The Old Guard fought the Ancients" needed a mechanism.** Canon says the Ancients are forces with *natures, not intentions* — you cannot stab Wrath. So what, exactly, did the Old Guard fight when they stood against an Ancient? This document answers that without breaking the rule.

The payoff: a layered bestiary, a war history with three distinct kinds of conflict, and an armory whose loot comes from *across* that whole spectrum — not all of it Old Guard, not all of it Ancient, much of it simply the spoils of a dangerous world.

## I. CANON TWEAKS — FLAGGED FOR SIGN-OFF

These are adjustments to existing canon. Each is marked **[ADJUSTABLE]** — none of this is locked until you say so. I am surfacing them rather than quietly rewriting, per our standing practice.

**1. Kronarch is mortal-origin, not an Ancient. [ADJUSTABLE]**
The boss art calls him an *"apocalyptic ancient titan,"* which reads as though he might *be* one of the Ancients. He is not, and the lore is stronger if this is crisp: Kronarch was a **mortal warlord-king** who gorged himself on stolen Ancient power during the Sundering and was warped into something titan-*scaled* but not titan-*kind*. He is what a man becomes when he reaches for the Ancients and they recoil. This is the entire reason Malachar's arc lands — *Malachar is unknowingly walking Kronarch's road.* If Kronarch were simply an Ancient, that mirror breaks. Recommendation: keep the word "titan" as a description of his scale; never call him an Ancient.

**2. The recurrence loop has a stated cause. [ADJUSTABLE]**
First Canon establishes that defeating Kronarch *re-seals* rather than kills him, and that he returns as Awakening levels rise. The mechanism: **the same Awakening Essence that stirs the Ancients also feeds Kronarch's stolen-essence body and erodes the null-sigil binding that holds him.** Every Awakening the players cause is, quietly, a withdrawal against the seal. This makes the recurrence *the player's own fault* in a way the world never quite tells them — which is exactly the dark register we want.

**3. The Iron Colossus has a reason to be hostile. [ADJUSTABLE]**
It is an Old Guard war-construct (canon). Gap: why does an Old Guard machine now fight Old Guard descendants? Ruling: the Colossus is **masterless, not corrupted by an enemy.** Its makers died in the Sundering before issuing a stand-down. It still runs its last standing order — *hold this ground, suffer no approach* — with no one left alive who knows the counter-sign. It is not evil. It is a loyal thing that was never told the war ended. (See §V, "The Wrought.")

**4. Monsters predate the Sundering. [ADJUSTABLE]**
Establishing that the beasts and broods of the Sundermarch are **native to Aldenmor and older than the Old Guard** — the world was always dark and full of teeth. The Sundering and the rising Awakening do not *create* the bestiary; they *worsen* it, spawning corrupted variants atop the existing population. This gives us two clean monster sources — the ancient native population, and the escalating Awakening-Touched layer — and the second one is a renewable content engine (see §VI).

## II. THE FOUR ORDERS OF BEING

Everything that walks, swims, festers, or grinds across the Sundermarch falls into one of four Orders. The Old Guard catalogued them by what it took to put them down.

### Order I — The Ancients
The four primal forces. Marching, Standing, Taking, Perceiving — and the named natures that grew from them, of which **Wrath** is the first the Old Guard ever recorded by name. They are not creatures. They cannot be slain, cornered, starved, or reasoned with. You do not fight an Ancient. You survive its attention. (How they were *opposed* at all: see §III, "The Tide-Holding," and §IV.)

### Order II — The Great Horrors
Titan-scale beings that *can* be killed, though it has taken armies and Ages to do it. Two kinds:
- **Primordials** — apex things that simply grew vast over the Age of Dawn and are *indifferent* to the Ancients. They are not servants of any primal force; they are just very old and very large. **The Sunken Leviathan** is the archetype.
- **The Swollen** — mortals or beasts bloated by Awakening Essence into something monstrous, hovering at the edge of becoming a Herald (§IV) without ever quite crossing over. **Gorehowl** is the archetype: a warlord so fed by endless slaughter that he is half a man and half a wound in the shape of Wrath.
- **Kronarch** stands alone — a Swollen mortal who deliberately reached for the Ancients themselves rather than merely feeding on Essence. The only one of his kind. (Pray it stays that way; see Malachar.)

### Order III — The Beasts & Broods
The native bestiary. Predators, vermin, swarms, and territorial horrors that were here before anyone and will be here after. They have no cosmological significance whatsoever — which is precisely why they matter. They are the world's *floor*, the thing every hero cuts their teeth on. The Old Guard's endless, unglamorous war was mostly fought here. (Signature creatures: §V.)

### Order IV — The Wrought
Made things. Constructs, war-engines, sigil-driven automata — most of them Old Guard work, a few older and stranger. They do not live and cannot truly die; they run until they are broken or until their commanding sigils decay into nonsense. A masterless Wrought is one of the most dangerous things in the Sundermarch, because it is doing exactly what it was told by people who are no longer alive to stop it. **The Iron Colossus** is the archetype.

> **The cross-cutting overlay — The Awakening-Touched.** Not a fifth Order but a *condition* that can afflict anything in II, III, and IV (and mortals besides). As Awakening Essence rises, it begins to overwrite living and made things alike — swelling them, lighting them with amber rot, bending their nature toward the primal act nearest them. This is the renewable corruption layer and the bridge to Order I. Heralds and manifestations of the Ancients live here. (See §VI.)

## III. HOW THE OLD GUARD WAGED WAR

The Old Guard fought three different wars at once, and most of its members never knew the third existed.

### The Long Watch — *against the Beasts, Broods, and feral Wrought*
The endless one. Culling broods, holding passes, burning nests, putting down a war-construct that slipped its leash. No songs were written about it. This is where ordinary fighters were forged and where the bulk of the Old Guard's dead are buried. **It is the war the player begins in.** Every starting zone is, in lore, a stretch of the Long Watch that was never fully won.

### The Named Campaigns — *against the Great Horrors*
The heroic war. When a Primordial woke or a Swollen rose, the Old Guard mustered its champions, and the survivors came home with relics, scars, and names that outlived them. Most of the Armory's legendary gear (§VII) is salvage, trophy, or reward from a Named Campaign. These are finite, mythic, and a thousand years gone — which is why their relics are *rare*.

### The Tide-Holding — *against the Ancients*
The secret war, and the answer to "how do you fight a force of nature."

**You cannot.** What you *can* do: when an Ancient stirs toward waking, its nature begins to bleed across the threshold of the world. That bleed takes form —

- **Heralds** — a mortal or beast so saturated by the Ancient's nature that it is overwritten, becoming a living avatar of the primal act. A warrior consumed past all return becomes a *Herald of Wrath.* Heralds are mortal in body and can be killed; killing one pushes the Ancient's presence back below the threshold for a time.
- **Manifestations** — monsters and phenomena birthed *directly* from the resonance, embodying the primal act in raw form. Tide-spawn. They are not born; they *condense.*

The Tide-Holding is the war to slay the Heralds and scatter the Manifestations fast enough to keep the Ancient's nature from fully crossing over — to hold the tide below the waterline until it recedes on its own. It is doomed by design; you never *win*, you only hold. This is what First Canon means by *"the Old Guard spent the Sundering holding lines rather than winning campaigns."* They were holding the tide.

**And here is the knife:** the player, by generating Awakening Essence, *raises the tide.* Every Awakening makes Heralds and Manifestations more frequent and more potent. The Old Guard held the line so the Ancients would stay asleep. The player is doing the opposite — and the game never stops them, because waking the world is the point.

## IV. THE ANCIENT OF WRATH (worked example)

Given as a template for how any Ancient should be developed when introduced.

**Primal act:** Taking-by-force. The first blow ever struck to seize rather than survive.
**Nature, not intention:** Wrath does not *want* anything. It is the standing fact of escalation — that violence, once begun, tends to grow. You do not anger Wrath; you *qualify* for it.
**Its tide, when it stirs:**
- *Manifestations* — **Cinderkin**, things that condense out of the air above a long enough massacre: man-shaped, slag-skinned, burning from within, made of nothing but the heat of the killing beneath them.
- *Heralds* — a mortal who has killed past the point of reason and kept going. **Gorehowl** is the nearest any living thing has come to becoming the Herald of Wrath without fully crossing over; he is the cautionary near-miss, perpetually one battle away.
**What it leaves behind:** *Wrath-slag* — cooled residue of a Manifestation, still warm centuries later. (See §VII, the Cinder-Cuff.)
**The planted question (do not answer):** the Old Guard texts disagree on whether Wrath was the *first* Ancient or merely the first they survived long enough to name.

## V. THE BESTIARY — SIGNATURE CREATURES

A handful developed deeply, spanning the Orders. Not all are Old Guard-relevant; most are simply the world being dangerous. These seed the Armory in §VII.

### The Sunken Leviathan — *Order II, Primordial* (existing canon, deepened)
A primordial of the Drowned Coast, older than the Sundering that drowned its hunting grounds. It is **not** an Ancient-thing and serves no primal force — it is simply the largest living creature in Aldenmor, a coiled mountain of armor-scale and sounding-flesh that was here long before mortals learned to fear the water. When the southern coast fell into the sea, the Leviathan inherited a kingdom of ruins and has slept in them ever since, surfacing only when the deep is disturbed. *The Old Guard fought it once, in a Named Campaign that cost a generation of champions and ended not in a kill but in a truce of exhaustion.* It was never slain. It was *let be.*
**Yields:** Leviathan sounding-horn, armor-scale, the cold oil of its lamps. (Trophy tier: **Aureate.**)

### Gorehowl, the Unfinished Herald — *Order II, Swollen* (existing canon, recast)
A warlord swollen by the Essence of his own endless butchery until he stands at the threshold of becoming the Herald of Wrath — and never tips over, because some last splinter of the man refuses to dissolve. He is more terrible for it: a Great Horror that is *almost* a force of nature, fighting to stay a person while everything in him burns toward Wrath. He does not lead an army anymore. He *is* the battle.
**Yields:** war-axe fragments still warm to the touch, blood that will not cool. (Trophy tier: **Aureate.**)

### The Mire-Brood — *Order III, native* (new)
Swamp-horrors of the Drowned Coast shallows — pale, many-limbed, egg-bound things that nest in the flooded ruins the Leviathan ignores. They have no significance, no master, no intelligence beyond hunger and territory. They are simply what fills a drowned land. They breed in numbers and are most dangerous in the dark. Every fighter who works the Drowned Coast knows the smell of a Brood-nest before they see it.
**Yields:** **mire-ichor** (a crafting reagent — see §VII), brood-chitin, the luminous sacs they use to lure prey. (Trophy tier: **Bronzed.**)

### The Ashen Stag — *Order III, native apex* (new)
The great predator of the Ironspine high country — not a deer but a thing that wears antlers, grey as cold ash, tall as a mounted rider, silent on scree where nothing should move silently. It is solitary, territorial, and patient, and it has killed more lone Old Guard scouts over the Ages than any Great Horror. There is nothing cosmological about it. It is just the most dangerous *animal* in the mountains, and the Heartmarch hunters who bring one down are remembered by name.
**Yields:** ash-antler, the grey hide, the heart-tendon prized by bowyers. (Trophy tier: **Argent.**)

### The Iron Colossus — *Order IV, Wrought* (existing canon, clarified)
See §I.3. A masterless Old Guard war-construct still executing its final standing order. Not corrupted — *abandoned.* It guards a ground whose strategic value died with the Age of Dawn, and it will let nothing approach, because the counter-sign that would stand it down is known to no living mouth. Fighting it is less a battle than an argument with a dead general.
**Yields:** sigil-plate, a fragment of its commanding core. (Trophy tier: **Aureate.**)

### The Glutbound — *Awakening-Touched overlay* (new — the renewable layer)
Not a species. A **condition.** When ambient Essence rises high enough in a region, ordinary beasts begin to swell — bones lengthening, amber light weeping from the seams of them, hunger sharpening into something that no longer needs to eat to keep killing. A Glutbound wolf, a Glutbound brood, a Glutbound stag: each is its base creature plus the rot of too much Awakening. They are the visible sign that the tide is rising. **This is the template for endless future variants** — any Order III or IV creature can be issued a Glutbound form, scaling with the Awakening meter, as ongoing content. (See §VI.)
**Yields:** Essence-rotted material — unstable, valuable, dangerous to carry. (Trophy tier: variable; rises with severity.)

## VI. THE ESCALATION ENGINE (design note, hold for content cadence)

The pieces above lock into a renewable loop, flagged here so it is on record and **not** spent prematurely:

- Rising Awakening → more **Glutbound** variants of existing beasts (cheap, frequent content).
- Higher still → **Manifestations** condense (tide-spawn; mid-tier events).
- Higher still → a **Herald** rises and must be put down (boss-tier event; re-suppresses the tide).
- The seal on **Kronarch** erodes in parallel, guaranteeing his recurrence (§I.2).

This means content can be released as *the tide rising* rather than as disconnected updates — every new monster is diegetically *caused* by the players' own progress. **Recommendation:** do not introduce Heralds or a second Swollen-into-Kronarch figure yet. Both are powerful late-game reveals. Holding them.

## VII. THE ARMORY — TEN ITEMS

Curated, full flavor, stats-ready hooks. Deliberately spread across every source: native trophies, Old Guard relics, a pre-Old Guard mystery, an Awakening drop, mundane-but-mythic gear, a Gauntlet reward, and one thread that brushes the Pano mystery without resolving it. Rarity uses the established colour tiers (White → Blue → Purple → Orange); trophies also carry a trophy tier (Aureate / Argent / Bronzed). **All stat hooks are [ADJUSTABLE]** — they're starting points for whoever owns the combat math.

---

**1. Gravewend, the Last Pitchfork** — *Weapon (polearm) · White-framed, legendary by history · mundane*
> No sigil. No edge worth the name. A farm tool from a Heartmarch village whose name is not written down, carried by a man whose name is not written down, who used it to kill a thing that should have killed him. The village is gone. The pitchfork is not.
*Hook: low base damage, but lands a flat bonus against Order III Beasts & Broods — it remembers what it was made to drive off.*
*Lore: this is the pitchfork the fighters' informal currency is named for. Every "Pitchfork" earned is a small joke about the peasant who outlived a monster with a hay fork. The joke is the only thing keeping his memory alive.*

**2. The Sealwright's Stylus** — *Tool / off-hand · Purple · Old Guard relic*
> The instrument used to inscribe null-sigil bindings — the script that holds a thing *closed.* Cold to the touch in a way that has nothing to do with temperature. The Old Guard made few of these and accounted for every one. This one is not on the ledger.
*Hook: enables or empowers binding/suppression effects; minor bonus to abilities that lock, root, or seal. Resonates faintly near Kronarch's prison — flavor flag for a quest hook.*
*Lore: the craft that sealed Kronarch in the Ironspine. That an unaccounted Stylus exists in the Age of Quiet should worry someone.*

**3. Sounding-Horn of the Sunken Leviathan** — *Trophy / consumable-charged · Orange · Aureate trophy · Order II*
> Cut from a Primordial that was never killed, only out-waited. It still holds one note. The Old Guard who took it never agreed on what the note *does* — only that the sea answered, the one time it was sounded.
*Hook: single-charge AoE on a long cooldown; thematically a summon/lure/quake. Powerful, scarce, irreplaceable — there is no second Leviathan.*
*Lore: spoil of a Named Campaign that ended in a truce of exhaustion, not a victory. Carrying it is carrying an unpaid debt.*

**4. Mire-Ichor** — *Crafting reagent · White/Blue (quantity item) · Order III*
> Pale, luminous, foul. Bled from the Brood-things of the drowned shallows. Useless on its own; the basis of half the alchemy on the Drowned Coast.
*Hook: stack-able reagent feeding into crafting/upgrade recipes. The workhorse drop — common, infinitely sink-able, the floor of the economy.*
*Lore: deliberately mundane. Not every drop is a legend. A world needs its raw materials, and the Brood provides them by the barrelful.*

**5. The Cinder-Cuff** — *Bracer · Purple → Orange (volatile) · Awakening drop*
> Forged from Wrath-slag — the cooled residue of a Manifestation, still faintly warm an Age later. It makes the wearer stronger and angrier in the same motion, and does not distinguish between the two.
*Hook: meaningful offensive bonus that scales with consecutive kills / combat momentum, paired with a downside that punishes disengagement — it wants you to keep swinging. A double-edged item, by nature.*
*Lore: a piece of the Ancient of Wrath's tide, cooled into wearable form. It is, very slightly, *trying to qualify you.** 

**6. The Vanguard's Cold Token** — *Trinket · Blue → Purple · planted mystery*
> An identification token of the Lost Vanguard — not the famous signet, which is gone with the man who wore it, but kin to it: the same unknown script worn nearly smooth, the same cold that does not warm in the hand. Whatever the Vanguard were, they marked themselves in a language no one alive can read.
*Hook: modest utility trinket; minor bonus to whatever stat tracks the "scout/explorer" fantasy. Its real value is the question it carries.*
*Lore: deliberately brushes the Pano thread without resolving it. **Do not, in any future content, let this token translate the script.** It exists to keep the mystery warm, not to spend it.*

**7. Sovereign's Tithe-Mark** — *Brand / cosmetic-stat hybrid · Orange · Gauntlet institutional*
> Granted by the Gauntlet, in the name of the founding pact with the Dragon Sovereign. It marks a fighter as having paid the tithe — the price of climbing. What, exactly, the Sovereign was promised in that first pact is recorded nowhere a fighter is allowed to read.
*Hook: prestige item gating or empowering Gauntlet progression; pairs with the Whelp-Warden-to-Dragon-Sovereign ladder. Status made wearable.*
*Lore: ties the loot economy to the Gauntlet's institutional identity and keeps the Dragon Sovereign's bargain a live, unanswered thread.*

**8. The Unworn Crown** — *Relic / head (mystery) · Orange · pre-Old Guard*
> Older than the Old Guard. Older than the Old Guard's *records* of older things. A circlet of a dark metal no smith now living can name, made for a head that no carving depicts. It has never, in any account, been worn. The Old Guard kept it not because they knew what it was, but because they were afraid to be the ones who found out.
*Hook: leave its effect *unrevealed* at first — a relic the player holds long before it does anything, with effects that unlock through a deep quest chain. A slow-burn mystery item.*
*Lore: the deep-time layer. Proof that Aldenmor had inhabitants and powers before even the Age of Dawn — a door First Canon left closed, here given a doorknob and nothing more.*

**9. Ash-Antler Greatbow** — *Weapon (bow) · Purple · Argent trophy · Order III*
> Strung on the heart-tendon of an Ashen Stag, tipped with its own grey antler. The Heartmarch hunters say a Stag-bow never loses the silence of the thing it was made from — that arrows loosed from it arrive before the sound does.
*Hook: ranged weapon with a stealth/first-strike bonus; bonus damage on opening shots or against unaware targets. A clean, beloved mid-tier weapon from a purely mundane kill.*
*Lore: a trophy of skill, not magic. The Ashen Stag has no cosmic weight — which is the point. The best gear isn't always carved from a god.*

**10. Colossus-Core Shard** — *Upgrade component / off-hand · Orange · Aureate trophy · Order IV*
> Pried from the commanding core of an Old Guard war-construct that never stood down. It still pulses with the last order it was given, a single fixed intent with no one left to amend it: *hold.* Set into gear, it lends that obstinacy to the wearer.
*Hook: defensive component — flat damage reduction, or a stand-your-ground / immovable effect; punishes the wearer slightly for retreating, mirroring the construct's refusal to yield ground.*
*Lore: salvage from the Iron Colossus. It is loyal, in the way a closed door is loyal. It does not know the war is over either.*

## VIII. PRINCIPLES CARRIED FORWARD

- **Not everything is Old Guard, and not everything is Ancient.** Of the ten items above, only three are Old Guard work and only two touch a primal force. The rest are trophies, mundane gear, a deep-time mystery, and a barrel of swamp ichor. The world is bigger than its heroes and its gods.
- **Mundane drops are load-bearing.** Mire-Ichor matters *because* it is ordinary. An economy needs a floor.
- **Mystery items must stay mysterious.** The Cold Token and the Unworn Crown exist to hold questions open. Resolving them cheaply would spend canon we cannot get back.
- **Corruption is renewable; Heralds are not.** The Glutbound layer can run forever. A Herald rising is a *rare, earned* event. Don't confuse the two.
- **Tone holds: dark, mythic, grave.** No item winks at the player except Gravewend — and even that joke is half an elegy.
