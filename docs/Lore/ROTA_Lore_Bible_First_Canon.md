# ROTA — Lore Bible: First Canon
*Companion to the Lore Handoff Document. Compiled 2026-06-08.*
*Status: Newly established canon — not yet in the codebase.*
*This document extends the Handoff; it supersedes nothing in it.*
*All proper nouns from the Handoff are preserved exactly.*

## I. THE WORLD

### Name
**Aldenmor.**

The word predates every living language. Old Guard scholars rendered it *"the old dark"* — not as a condemnation but as an acknowledgment: this world is not new, something great inhabited it before the current age, and the weight of that prior habitation never fully lifted. Aldenmor is not a kingdom or a continent. It is the world entire — everything beneath the sky that the Ancients once regarded.

### The Continent
**The Sundermarch.**

Named for the Sundering — the catastrophe that ended the Age of Dawn and divided the world's history into *before* and *after*. The Sundermarch is the landmass where the game takes place: iron-grey mountain ranges in the north, cultivated lowlands at its center, a ruined coastline to the south where old shorelines collapsed into a new and deeper sea. It is a fractured place. The fractures are old enough that most people have stopped noticing them. They are not old enough to have healed.

### The Two Ages

**The Age of Dawn** — the prior age. The age when the Ancients walked and the Old Guard stood. Its details are fragmentary; the Sundering destroyed most written record. What survives is ruins, the Gauntlet, the relics of the Old Guard's champions, and objects that hum with old power. The current civilization lives in the age after the Dawn. Most of its people have no idea what the Dawn was.

**The Age of Quiet** — the current age. The Ancients sleep. The Old Guard is gone. The Heartmarch kingdoms are alive, prosperous, and entirely unprepared for what the world is building toward. It has been called the Age of Quiet for two generations: quiet not because nothing happens, but because the things that matter most make no sound.

## II. THE ANCIENTS — COSMOLOGICAL ORIGIN

*Design principle honored: mystery is a feature. The origin below is mythic, not technical — enough to make the Ancients feel real, not enough to make them feel small.*

The Ancients did not arrive. They grew.

In the earliest epoch of Aldenmor — before mortal kingdoms, before even the oldest elven settlements — there was only the raw material of the world: iron in the mountains, water in the deep, blood in the first creatures that tore each other apart. From that tearing, from the first four primal acts of mortal existence, four presences thickened into being. Not gods. Not created beings. More like weather: they arose because the conditions of the world demanded something to arise.

**Wrath** emerged from the first army that ever marched — the moment mortal violence ceased to be individual and became *collective*. Every war fought since then is a continuation of that first marching. The Wrathfire is not rage. It is the momentum of a thousand unified wills turned toward destruction.

**Bulwark** emerged from the first wall that held — the moment something chose to *stand* rather than yield. Every defense that refused to break, every brotherhood that held the line when breaking would have been the easier choice, is Bulwark's echo. The Mountain does not attack. It endures until attack becomes irrelevant.

**Hoard** emerged from the first prize taken from another — the first moment a creature understood that what it held was more valuable for being denied to its enemy. Every gold coin, every territorial conquest, every drop of loot carried from a fallen enemy is Hoard's dominion. The Greed is not petty. It is the principle of accumulation itself.

**Discernment** emerged from the first moment a creature looked into the dark and understood what hid there. The first act of perception that preceded survival. Every hidden thing found, every secret named, every flaw revealed in an enemy's defense is Discernment's nature. The Veiled Eye does not see everything. It sees the thing that matters.

They did not speak to each other. They did not rule. They were — vast, indifferent, immeasurable. Mortals who encountered their influence called them gods, built temples, ascribed intentions. The Old Guard knew better: the Ancients are forces with natures, not intentions. You do not pray to Wrath. You become worthy of it, or you do not.

The Ancients required no worship. They required only that the world continue doing what it does: waging war, holding lines, taking prizes, finding what is hidden. Every act of sufficient magnitude generated Awakening Essence — the ambient resonance of mortal power accumulating. When the world grew too quiet, when the great wars ended and the Old Guard fell and Kronarch's armies collapsed into dust, there was not enough signal remaining to sustain them. They sank. They did not die. They wait.

The question the game never fully answers: *did they choose to withdraw, or were they forced?* The Old Guard's surviving texts hold both positions. The Hall of the Ancients will record which version proved true.

## III. THE SUNDERING

The catastrophe that ended the Age of Dawn was not a natural disaster. It was a war.

Kronarch — a warlord-king of the late Age of Dawn — marshaled every mortal army in the Sundermarch behind a single ambition: seize the Ancients. Bind them. Rule the world through them. His scholars had studied enough Old Guard doctrine to understand that the Ancients were not gods beyond reach. They were forces that responded to accumulated power. He reasoned that enough power, concentrated in enough hands and directed with enough will, could compel them to answer.

He was partially right. The Ancients responded.

They did not respond the way he intended.

Rather than be bound, they withdrew. Not flight — withdrawal. They pulled their power inward, sank below the threshold of mortal perception, and left Kronarch's campaign to win against nothing. His armies had ridden on a tide of Awakening Essence drawn from the massive conflict they caused; without the Ancients sustaining that current, the tide retreated. The armies collapsed. The great structures Kronarch had built to channel Ancient power shattered or sank. The southern coastline, undermined by the geological resonance of the Ancients' withdrawal, fell into the sea. The Drowned Coast was born.

Kronarch survived. He was too vast — too saturated with stolen power — for simple death. What remained of the Old Guard, who had spent the Sundering holding lines rather than winning campaigns, cornered him in the north and sealed him. They drove him into the Ironspine Mountains and bound him in a fortress of null-sigil work, where his influence could not escape outward and nothing could reach him to release him inward.

The seal holds.

For now.

## IV. THE OLD GUARD

The institution that outlasted the Age of Dawn, barely.

It was not founded at the Dawn's height — it was founded after the Sundering, by survivors: scattered armies, scholars who fled Kronarch's advance, the champions who had carried Awakening Essence long enough to understand what it was. They gathered in the ruins of the frontier territory and made a single collective decision: *remember.* Remember the Ancients. Remember the Sundering. Remember what caused the Ancients to withdraw. Ensure the world never forgot so thoroughly that no one would know to wake them again.

The Old Guard built fortifications along the Ruined Frontier — the edge of the territory Kronarch's war had most devastated. They developed sigil-work: the binding technology that captures a creature's resonance signature and allows it to be summoned into combat again. They trained champions — the Lost Vanguards — the advance soldiers who pushed deepest into hostile territory, accumulated Awakening Essence through relentless combat, and reported what they found.

The Gauntlet was their proving institution, designed to generate concentrated Essence through tournament combat regardless of whether the kingdoms were at war or at peace.

The Iron Colossus was their masterwork construct: a siege-scale guardian animated through techniques derived from Ancient power signatures, built from iron saturated with Ancient resonance, assembled in the Ironspine forges from materials that had been through the Sundering. It was meant to serve as both protector and weapon. It was never fully controllable. The bindings that kept it quiescent required constant renewal by scholars who knew the Old Guard's methods. When the Order fell, those renewals stopped.

The Old Guard fell two generations ago. The records that survive are contradictory and incomplete. The Keep shattered. The Colossus's bindings degraded. The Frontier became ruins. The specific cause is disputed — internal collapse, sabotage, Malachar's early operations. What is not disputed: no institution in the current world maintains the Old Guard's knowledge. The Order is gone. What it left behind are ruins, relics, and one undead scholar who chose his condition rather than let the knowledge die.

## V. PANO — THE LOST VANGUARD

Pano was the Old Guard's last great champion.

His rank, Lost Vanguard, was the Order's highest field designation. The name was not given in mourning — it was given in acknowledgment: these soldiers were always ahead of the armies, ahead of the intelligence, ahead of the maps. They went where there was no path. They were lost from the perspective of anyone who needed to find them. From their own perspective, they were simply forward.

In the Old Guard's final season, Pano was advancing through the eastern Frontier when the Keep fell. He did not know — communication over that distance took days. He kept advancing. His mission: reach the eastern edge of Kronarch's seal in the Ironspine range, assess whether the null-sigil binding was degrading, and return with a report. The Old Guard's scholars had recorded trace anomalies in the ambient Essence readings from the north. Someone needed to look.

He did not return.

His gear was recovered later, scattered across the ruins — as if he had made his last stand over miles, fighting something that wore him down from the outside in. The helm was recovered a mile closer to the Frontier's center than the boots. He kept moving. The gear still hums with Awakening Essence because the Old Guard intentionally infused its champions with it. The Essence does not dissipate with time or distance. It waits, the way the Ancients wait, for something worthy of carrying it again.

What Pano found — whether he reached the seal, whether his report would have changed the Order's final decisions — is unknown. He is not simply a dead hero. He is a question the world did not get to answer before the Order collapsed. The Conscript who earns the full set of his gear has, in effect, walked the distance Pano walked. Whether they find a different end to the road remains to be seen.

One detail of note: Pano's Signet bears an inscription on the inner band, worn past reading by the time it was recovered. Every scholar who has examined it agrees on one thing — it is not in any language the Old Guard formally used. It may predate the Order.

## VI. LORD MALACHAR

Malachar is not ancient. He is the present threat — the villain of the current age, not the prior one.

He is a mage-warlord who rose in the power vacuum left by the Old Guard's collapse: a scholar-soldier who recognized that the Frontier's ruins were not simply rubble but an archive. Old Guard fortifications are saturated with residual Awakening Essence — what Malachar calls *the dust* — a persistent trace that never fully dissipates from stone that has been consecrated with it over generations. His soldiers carry harvest-instruments into the ruins. The Arcane Dust they collect is genuine raw material from the Age of Dawn.

Malachar believes — correctly, in the narrow sense — that enough accumulated Awakening Essence can force an Awakening event without waiting for the natural cycle of mortal combat to build the server-wide meter. He is trying to shortcut a process the Old Guard spent generations carefully managing. He has the right destination and the wrong method.

He does not know he is repeating Kronarch's attempt. He has read enough of the Old Guard's fragments to understand the goal; he has not read enough to understand the cost.

He is not a monster in the conventional sense. He is a man who has correctly identified that the Ancients' dormancy is a wound the world needs to heal from, who has concluded that waiting for that healing to happen naturally is a luxury the world cannot afford, and who has decided that the blood and wreckage his operations leave behind is an acceptable price. He has thought about this carefully. He has reached the wrong answer.

His Vanguard is the forward element of his army, pushing into the Ruined Frontier to secure harvesting sites and clear resistance. They are professional soldiers operating under clear orders. They are the first organized threat a new Conscript encounters, and they are substantially better equipped than bandits.

## VII. THE IRON COLOSSUS

A construct of the Old Guard, not a creature and not a god.

It was built in the second generation of the Order's existence, when the Old Guard had recovered enough of the Age of Dawn's technical knowledge to attempt the animation of large-scale constructs. The Colossus was assembled in the Ironspine forges from materials accumulated over decades of expeditions into ruin sites: iron saturated with Ancient resonance, stone from fortifications that had stood through the Sundering, binding-metals recovered from locations where the Ancients' presence had been most concentrated. Everything that went into its construction had been through something.

It was designed as a guardian and a weapon. A threshold defender for the Keep in extreme circumstances. A force-projection asset when the Old Guard needed to meet a threat at a scale no human army could match. The sigil-bindings that controlled it were complex, layered, and required regular renewal by a rotating team of the Order's most experienced scholars.

When the Keep fell and that team dissolved, the bindings entered progressive degradation.

The Colossus does not think. But it responds. It responds to Awakening Essence the way a compass responds to north — orienting, orienting, orienting toward the accumulation of power in its territory. As Malachar's harvesting operations disturb the Frontier's ambient Essence readings, and as the first Conscripts' combat activity begins to build power in the ruins around the Keep's location, the Colossus stirs. The quest node title is accurate: it *awakens.* Not in anger. Not with purpose. In response to exactly the signal it was built to respond to.

When it rises, it fights as it was built to fight — against any concentration of power in its territory. It does not distinguish between enemies of the Old Guard and inheritors of the Old Guard's mission. That distinction required scholars who are gone.

The Iron Shards it leaves behind are Old Guard construction material. They retain resonance. They are not debris — they are raw material waiting for someone who knows what to do with it.

## VIII. GUILD BOSSES

### Gorehowl the Warlord

A mortal. A very capable and very dangerous mortal, but a mortal.

He rose from the mercenary companies that formed in the power vacuum after the Old Guard collapsed and the Frontier went unguarded. There were dozens of such companies in the first generation after the collapse; most fought each other, or were picked off by whatever emerged from the ruins, or simply dissolved. Gorehowl recognized early that the fractured companies would either unify or be eliminated piecemeal. He unified them. By force, primarily. By effectiveness, occasionally. By the simple fact that battles fought under his command tended to end with his side alive and paid.

His territory is the western Ruined Frontier, which he has fortified into a personal domain over two decades of careful consolidation. He has no interest in Ancients, Old Guard scholarship, or Malachar's ambitions. He does not think about the Age of Dawn. He thinks about territory, logistics, and threat assessment. Guilds that push into the western Frontier represent a threat. Threats are met.

His name is not Gorehowl. His soldiers started calling him that after a battle in which he personally held a narrow pass against a force four times his size, howling the entire time. He did not object to the name. He has never offered them another one.

### The Sunken Leviathan

Not a sea creature. Not originally.

The Leviathan was a titan-category predator from the Ironspine range — something old enough and large enough that Kronarch's forces did not attempt to kill it during the Sundering campaign. Instead, his scholars bound it. They used corrupted Old Guard sigil techniques — stolen, imperfectly understood, applied to a creature that was not designed to hold such bindings. It held, adequately. The Leviathan was driven south with Kronarch's armies and fought in the lowland campaigns adjacent to the coast.

When the southern shoreline collapsed into the sea during the Ancients' withdrawal, the Leviathan was in the lowlands. It drowned with the coast.

It did not die.

It is far too large and too saturated with the stolen binding-work to end by drowning. Over centuries it adapted to the dark water, or the dark water adapted around it — the distinction is difficult to establish from the outside. It does not seek the surface. It exists in the deep water of the Drowned Coast, and the coastline has been uninhabitable since the Sundering not primarily because of the flooding but because of what waits beneath it.

The Leviathan does not hate. It does not plan. It has been waiting — for the binding to release, for the water to recede, for something familiar to approach. It has been waiting long enough that waiting is no longer distinguishable from existence.

### Kronarch, World-Ender

The architect of the Sundering.

He was not always called World-Ender. His epithet before the catastrophe was the Sovereign Warlord — a title given by his armies at the height of his campaign, when he had unified the Sundermarch's military power under a single will for the first time in recorded history. *World-Ender* was not given by admirers. It was given by the people who watched the southern coastline fall into the sea, understood that the man who started this was still alive and sealed in the north, and concluded that an accurate accounting of his capacity was more useful than a comforting one.

In his seal in the Ironspine Mountains, Kronarch endures. He does not sleep — not the way the Ancients sleep. He is conscious and contained. Centuries of containment have not diminished him. They have made him patient. He was not a patient man before the sealing. He is patient now. There is a difference between patience earned and patience imposed, and Kronarch has had centuries to understand the distinction.

The seal weakens with every Awakening event. The same Awakening Essence that stirs the Ancients stirs Kronarch — because he is saturated with Ancient power he stole and was never forced to return. Every time the server's Awakening meter fills, every time the world's combat and conflict builds toward an Awakening, Kronarch rises with it.

Guilds do not kill Kronarch. The confrontation re-seals him. It forces the power back down to a level the old bindings can hold again. He will return, because the cost of waking the world up is that everything the world sealed wakes up too.

His epithet is not exaggeration. He came within reach of ending the world. He still believes he can.

## IX. THE GAUNTLET — IN-WORLD IDENTITY

The Gauntlet was founded by the Old Guard in the middle period of the Age of Quiet, after the Sundering and before the Order's collapse — built in recognition of a problem the Order faced: mortal wars are irregular, and the Ancients' dormancy was being sustained by the world's quiet. The Gauntlet was a mechanism to ensure that concentrated Awakening Essence continued to be generated regardless of whether the kingdoms were at war or at peace. A recurring tournament that could be relied upon to produce the kind of escalating, increasingly skilled combat that produces genuine Essence accumulation.

The trophies — Aureate, Argent, Bronzed — were made from metals provided as founding materials by the Order's charter. The Aureate Trophy carries a trace of the Ancient Hoard's recognition; to win it is to be acknowledged by the Ancient of accumulation as the greatest accumulator of power in a given cycle. This is not metaphor. The trophies still carry that trace. It is why they grant the legion-power bonuses they grant.

The draconic hierarchy of Gauntlet bosses is structural, not incidental.

The Dragon Sovereign is a bound dragon — old enough to have witnessed the Ancients walking, old enough to have heard them move through the world in the age before language. The Old Guard struck a pact with it. In exchange for the Dragon Sovereign's cooperation as the Gauntlet's capstone challenger — the ultimate test against which the Order could measure its champions — the Order promised the dragon something. The records do not say what. The Dragon Sovereign holds to the pact without explanation or elaboration. It has been at the top of the hierarchy since the Gauntlet's founding. It waits.

The lower bosses — Whelp Warden, Drake Sentinel, Wyrm Vanguard, Elder Drake, Ancient Wyrm — are the Dragon Sovereign's lineage: its offspring and the offspring of its offspring, extending down through generations too numerous to count from the outside. They do not speak. They fight with a precision that suggests they are not simply obstacles but gradations — each one testing a more refined capacity than the one before it, as if the Dragon Sovereign designed an escalation toward itself.

The Gauntlet has outlasted the Old Guard. It operates as if the Order still governed it, because the Dragon Sovereign still governs the dragon hierarchy and the founding trophies still carry their Ancient trace. No living person knows how to close it. Most assume it has always existed. It will continue to exist as long as the Dragon Sovereign holds to a pact no one alive was present to witness.

## X. THE UNDEAD — RACIAL ORIGIN

Not a natural race. Not a blessed one. Not created by any external power.

The Undead are those who refused death — mortals of the Age of Dawn who bound their spirits to their remains using corrupted Old Guard ritual work. The first practitioners were scholars and soldiers of the Order who foresaw the collapse, understood that human lifespans were too short to carry the necessary knowledge through the Age of Quiet, and chose an alternative. The ritual was Old Guard in origin, adapted from the same sigil-work the Order used to bind creatures for the Gauntlet — repurposed, with incomplete understanding of the long-term effects, to bind a mortal soul to its own body.

The ritual worked. They survived.

The flaw was discovered over the following generations.

The binding that preserved them also severed their connection to Awakening Essence. The Undead cannot contribute to the Ancients' stirring. They are, in the cosmological framework the Old Guard used, *completed* — the mortal part of the equation is done, but the divine part of the cycle never arrives for them. They carry everything the Order knew and cannot do what the Order could do. They are archives that cannot act.

The early Undead — those who chose the ritual with full understanding of what they were giving up — carry their condition with something resembling equanimity. They knew the cost. They paid it intentionally. **Morvath the Unliving** is among these. He was a junior scholar of the Old Guard in its final season, assigned to the eastern frontier documentation project under Pano's operational command. When it became clear the Order would not survive the collapse, Morvath chose the ritual out of obligation: someone had to remember. He is old enough that the Ironspine Mountains he walked through as a living man have substantially different shapes than the ones he looks at now. Whether what he carries in his cursed memory could change the outcome of the current age is not a question he has been asked by someone he trusts.

Shadow Acolytes are the lesser Undead — those who chose the ritual for cruder reasons (fear of death, desire for continued power) and received less of the knowledge and more of the curse. They channel fragmented dark wisdom because they are drawing on the Awakening Essence they absorbed in life, imperfectly and without understanding. They are dangerous the way a damaged mechanism is dangerous: not because of intent, but because of incomplete function.

## XI. THE "DAWN" IN DAWN VANGUARD

The Dawn in Dawn Vanguard is a direct reference to the Age of Dawn, and the formation is a direct inheritance from Old Guard fighting doctrine.

The Dawn Vanguard configuration — three generals, three troops, mobile and self-sufficient — was the Old Guard's standard engagement structure for its champion-class units. It was the formation in which the Lost Vanguards operated when they fought as a unit rather than individually. Three generals provided command redundancy: the formation could lose two leaders in the field and still function. Three troops provided the tactical mass necessary for sustained engagements in hostile territory without resupply. It was designed for exactly the kind of deep-Frontier operation Pano exemplified.

The formation survived the Old Guard's collapse in textual form, preserved in fragments of doctrine that circulated among the mercenary companies and military scholars of the Age of Quiet. For most of that period it was theoretical — a description of how the Order fought, useful for historians but not for armies. Its availability as a formation in the game's current era reflects the broader truth of the opening premise: the world is slowly recovering prior knowledge. The Conscript who graduates to the Dawn Vanguard formation is using techniques that were dormant for two generations. This is not incidental. It is the game's central arc expressed in mechanical terms.

## XII. GEOGRAPHY — COMPLETE REFERENCE

**Aldenmor** — the world.

**The Sundermarch** — the continent. Named for the Sundering.

**The Heartmarch** — the interior lowlands. The current mortal kingdoms. Prosperous, stable, largely ignorant of the Age of Dawn. Not a current game zone. The Ruined Frontier is the Heartmarch's northern buffer — the line between comfortable ignorance and the evidence that something larger is returning.

**The Ruined Frontier** — Chapter 1's zone. The borderland of what was once the Old Guard's primary operational territory. Broken fortifications, dormant constructs, Malachar's harvesting operations, and the Keep's outer ruins. Where the game begins. Its border to the south is the Heartmarch; its border to the north is the Ironspine foothills. It runs east to west across the full width of the continent's upper middle.

**The Dragon's Shadow** — Chapter 2's zone. A highland plateau east of the Frontier, named for the shadow cast by the Sovereign's Peak — the mountain the Dragon Sovereign claimed as its territory in the founding pact. Malachar's Vanguard has pushed into this region in advance of the player's arrival; his forces control the approaches. The plateau itself is ancient, geologically unusual, and largely unoccupied by anything that lives at ground level.

**The Ancient Keep** — Chapter 3's zone. The ruins of the Old Guard's primary fortification. Not incidentally named — this is the Keep: the one Pano's report was meant to return to, the one whose fall ended the Order, the one the Iron Colossus was built to defend. The ruins are extensive, structurally complex, and dangerous in ways that extend beyond the physical.

**The Ironspine Mountains** — the northern range. Cold, impassable in heavy winter, rich in iron-saturated stone. Kronarch's seal is here, in the deep interior. The Old Guard's founding forges are here, long cold. The mountain range functioned as a natural barrier during the Sundering — Kronarch's armies could not move through it at the pace the war demanded, which is partly why the northern interior survived the war's worst effects.

**The Drowned Coast** — the southern shoreline. Collapsed into the sea during the Sundering's geological resonance. Uninhabited. The Sunken Leviathan's territory. The depth of the water increases faster than it should as one moves away from the shore — the coastline dropped rather than eroded. Old port ruins are visible at low tide in some locations.

**Sovereign's Peak** — a single mountain in the eastern highland, incorporated into the founding agreement between the Old Guard and the Dragon Sovereign. The mountain itself is unremarkable except that the Dragon Sovereign regards it as its own and has for longer than the Order existed. The Gauntlet's founding records describe it as the agreed boundary: the Dragon Sovereign operates within the Gauntlet; the Gauntlet operates around the Peak.

## XIII. MAGIC ITEMS — EXTENDED LORE FRAGMENTS

### Sigils
Sigil-work was the Old Guard's most significant technical achievement, developed in the Order's first generation and refined continuously until the collapse.

A sigil is an imprint — a binding construct that captures the resonance signature of a powerful creature or construct at a moment of significant power expression, and preserves that signature in physical form. The sigil is then an activation key: a way to recall the captured entity into combat state on demand. The Old Guard developed the capture and activation techniques; the anchor materials came from whatever the Frontier's ruins could provide.

The Iron Sigil was made while the Colossus was still bound and operational — a deliberate preservation of its combat signature by Old Guard scholars who understood that the bindings might someday fail and wanted a controlled alternative. Malachar's Sigil was made differently: Malachar performed a modified version of the capture ritual on himself, deliberately, creating a sigil of his own essence as a tactical instrument. A man who has made a sigil of himself has, in some technical sense, arranged to be summonable again after defeat. What he intends with this arrangement has not been made clear.

### Pinnacle Magics
Luminary's Echo, Archon's Decree, Ancient's Wrath, Elder Resonance, Eternal Aspect — these are not spells or techniques. They are the authentic expressions of power at each respective tier: what it looks, sounds, and feels like when something operating at Ancient-level interacts with the world. The Old Guard knew the tiers existed. They knew the names. They did not presume to define the expressions, because no mortal in the Age of Quiet had reached those tiers. The first to reach each level will discover, in the process of achieving it, what the expression actually is. The name is given in advance. The content is always new, because each person who achieves it carries a different accumulated weight toward that achievement.

### Wrath of the Ancients / Blessing of the Ancients
Not separate magics. Two faces of the same force, differentiated by what the recipient has earned.

The Gauntlet's rank-1 aura is Wrath because the top challenger has earned the full, unmediated attention of the Ancient of war. No interpretation, no Old Guard intermediation — the direct thing. Ranks 2–10 receive Blessing: formidable, but filtered. The Old Guard's founding documents for the Gauntlet describe the distinction carefully. They considered both sacred. They considered the difference important.

### Impending Doom
A purple-rarity magic, available only in Nightmare difficulty.

The Old Guard had a name for the sensation that precedes an Ancient's full stirring: the moment when something vast turns its attention toward you. They called it *the Doom* — not as a curse word but as a technical term, meaning the feeling of being within range of something that operates at Ancient scale. They considered it the closest most mortals would ever come to direct perception of an Ancient. The item is a weaponized fragment of that sensation: the feeling of something terrible about to arrive, directed outward at an enemy.

### Gems
Not rare stones. Crystallized Awakening Essence.

During the Ancients' withdrawal at the Sundering, the force of that withdrawal was so concentrated in certain locations that the ambient Essence did not dissipate — it compressed. It hardened, over decades, into physical form. The resulting crystals are scattered throughout the Sundermarch, most buried at depths that require either significant excavation or significant luck to reach. Those that surface are genuine fragments of the Age of Dawn's power, preserved in physical form and waiting for a purpose. The Old Guard's economy treated them as the highest denomination of material value, above gold, above land. A gem was a piece of the age that came before.

### Gold
The Old Guard's economy was gold-heavy for a practical reason the kingdoms of the Heartmarch inherited without understanding: the Awakening Essence cycle that Hoard governs tends to manifest materially as wealth alongside combat. War produces gold. The Order conducted enormous amounts of combat. The relationship between fighting and gold was not coincidental in the Age of Dawn — it was cosmological. Current kingdoms use gold as currency because the practice outlasted the explanation by two generations.

## XIV. CURRENCIES — BRIEF NOTES

**Gold** — material wealth, spoils of war, the mortal expression of Hoard's domain. See above.

**Gems** — crystallized Awakening Essence. The rarest material denomination. See above.

**Guild Sigils** — not Old Guard sigil-work; the name is inherited. They are the internal currency of the guild institution: proof of participation, denominated in collective achievement. Guilds issue them; guilds spend them. The form is ancient; the specific implementation is mortal.

**Gauntlet Tokens** — the Old Guard's tournament currency, unchanged since the Gauntlet's founding. The institution has never closed and has never needed to re-denominate. A Gauntlet Token is functionally as old as the tournament.

**Pitchforks** — the name given by Gauntlet fighters to the secondary currency earned at higher ranks. Not a name the Old Guard assigned — an informal designation that emerged from the fighters themselves and persisted because no official body was left to correct it. The likely origin: a high-Gauntlet-rank fighter receiving a currency token from a tournament built by warlords and scholars made a joke about farmers being awarded tools of the land for proving they could fight. The name stuck. It has been the name for longer than anyone living can remember.

## XV. THE RISE — COSMOLOGICAL FRAMEWORK

The game's central mechanic, understood through the lore:

Every act of combat, every completed quest, every guild raid generates Awakening Essence — the ambient resonance of mortal power accumulating. This is not metaphor. In the Age of Dawn, this accumulation was constant and vast; it was the signal that kept the Ancients conscious. When the signal dropped below the threshold — after the Sundering, after the Old Guard's collapse, after the age of great wars ended — the Ancients sank.

The Awakening Essence meter filling on the server is the world's signal level rising again. Players are not worshipping the Ancients or petitioning them. They are generating the conditions under which the Ancients wake naturally — the way the presence of sufficient heat naturally produces fire.

When an Ancient stirs, the world changes. The creature that appears is not the Ancient itself — the Ancients do not have physical forms in the mortal sense. It is the Ancient's *attention* given a shape the mortal world can fight against: a manifestation of what it means for Wrath, or Bulwark, or Hoard, or Discernment to fully orient toward a world that has called it back.

The community stages of an Awakening event — Stirred, Roused, Enraged, Sundered, Vanquished, Annihilated — describe the Ancient's transition from dormancy to full activity to the point where the community has generated enough collective force to push it back to equilibrium. The Ancient is not destroyed by this process. It is stabilized — held at a level of activity the world can sustain, rather than the full unmediated force of an Ancient operating without restraint.

This is what the Old Guard understood and what Malachar does not: the goal is not to force an Awakening. The goal is to be ready for one.

## XVI. WORLD STATE AT GAME OPENING

Two generations since the Old Guard fell.

The Heartmarch kingdoms are stable, prosperous, and entirely unprepared for what the world is building toward. The Ruined Frontier is contested — Malachar's harvesting operations versus whoever else has business in the ruins — and the contested nature of that territory is the first sign that the Age of Quiet is ending. Wars happen in the Heartmarch. What is happening in the Frontier is different. It is not a war over territory. It is a war over something the combatants don't fully understand yet.

Malachar's forces push forward. The Iron Colossus stirs in its dormancy, responding to the Essence accumulation beginning in the ruins around the Keep's location. Travelers from the northern passes report sounds from the Ironspine interior that no one from the south recognizes. Morvath the Unliving has not spoken to a mortal he trusted in forty years.

Into this: the player. A Conscript. A draftee in a war that does not yet have a clear enemy or a clear purpose. They start at the Frontier's edge with a Conscript helm and a Draft Horse that occasionally charges with surprising force, and the ruins around them are the remains of an order that understood everything currently at stake and was destroyed before it could use that understanding.

Everything in the world is a message. The work of rising from Conscript to Eternal is, in part, the work of becoming someone who can read it.

## XVII. ADDENDUM — PHASE-LATER NOTES

*Brief anchors for items deferred from the critical and important gaps. These are not fully developed — they are directions, not destinations.*

**Elven History:** The Elves were present in the Age of Dawn and were not principal combatants in the Sundering — they occupied territory east of the main campaign routes and survived the collapse largely intact. They remember more than they say. Sylvaire is not unusual for an Elf; Elves tend to know more than the situation calls for. Their role in the approaching Awakening is not yet defined.

**Political Structure:** The Heartmarch contains several mortal kingdoms of varying size and ambition. Their politics are not the game's concern. The lore establishes them as backdrop — real, functional, and about to become irrelevant as the Awakening approaches.

**The Ancients Before Dormancy:** In the Age of Dawn, the Ancients were not worshipped in any organized way — they were too vast and too indifferent for worship to be the right word. The closest equivalent: acknowledgment. The Old Guard acknowledged them. Most mortals simply lived inside their influence without naming it. The temples that exist in the Heartmarch to "the four powers" are pale echoes of something that was never actually religious.

**Sigils — Further Development:** The sigil system has unexplored depth. The question of whether a sigil can be made of an Ancient — whether the Awakening Essence system itself is a form of sigil-work operating at cosmic scale — is not addressed in current lore and should not be addressed prematurely. It is a door. Leave it closed for now.

**Future Ancients:** The fifth, sixth, and subsequent Ancients introduced through The Rise over the game's lifetime are not named here. Each should be developed at the time of its introduction, drawing on the cosmological framework established above: what primal act does this Ancient embody, and when in the world's history did it first arise?
