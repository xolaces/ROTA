# ROTA — Faction Dossier: The Iron Weir

## Overview
The Iron Weir is the closest thing Aldenmor has to a legitimate frontier power. It is not a kingdom, not a proper nation, and not particularly interested in becoming one. It is an assembly of broken keeps, veteran families, old-guard remnants, caravan lords, and frontier wardens who survived the Sundering by learning not to pretend the world could be made gentle.

They do not speak in grand ideals. They speak in terms of walls, routes, weather, supply, and blood. To the Iron Weir, civilization is a series of hard choices made under fire.

---

## I. Core Identity
The Weir is defined by one doctrine: hold the line until the line becomes a place.

Its members believe the frontier is not empty land, but a living wound: a stretch of old war, old ruin, and old menace that must be watched continuously. They view every town, courier road, and watchtower as a deliberate act of civilization. Every failed hold is not just a loss of land; it is a moral wound against the world.

The Weir is deeply suspicious of miracle cults, relic speculation, and any movement that tries to speak of the Ancients as if they were friends. The Ancients are not allies. They are conditions. The Weir understands that only too well.

---

## II. Political Makeup
The Iron Weir is a confederation of local powers, not a singular state. Its structure is a layered web:

- keep-blood houses from ruined frontier strongholds
- road wardens and caravan escorts who secure the routes
- veteran old-guard officers whose ranks survived the fall
- sigil engineers who maintain forgotten defensive systems
- quartermasters who keep the region fed and armed

This structure gives the Weir tactical depth and real social weight. It is not a glamorous faction. It is a practical one.

---

## III. Symbols and Rituals
- Symbol: a broken ring bound by black iron
- Banner: grey field, black clasp, blood-red line at the seam
- Ritual phrase: “No gate shall be unkept.”

Their rituals are simple and severe. The Weir does not celebrate saints or gods. It remembers the men and women who held a pass long enough to allow others to live. It performs funerary rites for watchtower keepers, wall-repairs for named falls, and oath-bound vows in the shadow of old frontier walls.

---

## IV. Commanders

### Marshal Veyra Dhal
The face of the Weir's strategic command. Veyra is not beloved. She is feared for her calm and respected for her discipline. She has built the Weir's roads into a military reality. She sees the frontier not as a place to conquer, but as a machine that must stay running.

### Captain Oren Hest
A veteran wall commander whose tactics are based on delaying traps, controlling elevation, and forcing enemies to fight in dead ground. He has survived too many campaigns to believe in courage alone.

### Sella Thorn
The Weir's quartermaster-general. The supplies of a frontier power are often more important than the soldiers themselves. Sella can turn a starving town into a fortress with a single secure convoy.

### Aric Vore
A blacksmith-soldier whose armor and weapons are nearly all derived from old-guard salvage and field repair. When he tells a man to stand, he means it in a way that makes the soldier feel his own bones.

### Teren Sclav
A road-warden who knows every safe pass across the Ironspine's outer ridges. He can navigate the old frontier better than most map-makers and is more feared than he is respected.

### Elowen Crest
An old-guard sigil engineer who maintains defensive arrays and warding nodes along the frontier. Her work is grim, practical, and occasionally horrifying in its effectiveness.

### Ser Vhal Nair
A veteran of the Long Watch who refuses to call himself a hero. He is obsessed with making the old war equal to the new one, and that obsession has made him a reliable leader in the field.

---

## V. Rivalries and Conflicts

### Malachar's Vanguard
The Weir sees Malachar's operations as a violent and absurd shortcut. They believe he is treating ruin as harvest, and that the world will react badly to such a philosophy. The Weir does not hate Malachar personally, but they consider his methods to be a profound misunderstanding of cost.

### The Dawnward Choir
The Choir's obsession with being recognized by the Ancients unsettles the Weir deeply. They think the Choir mistakes resonance for intention, and they are not wrong.

### The Dusk Ledger
The merchants of the Dusk Ledger want to treat relics as trade goods. The Weir sees that as a method of turning memory into a market and condemns it.

---

## VI. Why They Matter
The Iron Weir matters because they are one of the few factions with a genuine understanding of the frontier as a living system. They maintain walls, roads, and defense lines that are not symbolic; they are survival infrastructure.

If the world tips toward greater Awakening, the Iron Weir is the faction most likely to become the actual backbone of the region. They are not the most romantic force in ROTA, but they are the most durable.

---

## VII. Game Use Cases
- frontier defense quests
- escort contracts
- city siege or hold-the-line raids
- relic salvage protection
- fortress and watchtower content
- faction rivals for Dawnward and Malachar-aligned operations

---

## VIII. Expansion Hooks
The Weir is perfect ground for a wider arc in which the frontier itself begins to transform under Awakening pressure. Their roads, towers, and pass-strongholds become increasingly unstable, and their hold on the land starts to look less like prudence and more like a ritual of refusal.

This makes them a natural counterweight to the more mystical and destabilizing factions of the world.
