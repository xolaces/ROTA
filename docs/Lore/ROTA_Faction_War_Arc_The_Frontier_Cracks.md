# ROTA — Faction War Arc: The Frontier Cracks

## Overview
The frontier does not crack at a single point. It cracks in a chain of contested holds, broken roads, and ruined watchpoints that no one can agree are still worth defending.

The war now pulling across the Sundermarch is not yet a formal campaign. It is a series of tactical disputes, quiet assassinations, salvage seizures, and escalating resonance events all tied to a single strategic fact: the old frontier is beginning to move.

The Iron Weir wants to hold its lines and keep the world stable enough to survive. The Dawnward Choir wants to force the world to listen to the deeper pulse beneath it. The Dusk Ledger wants to monetize the old things before the world understands what they are. Malachar's Vanguard wants to turn the fracture into a machine for extraction and power.

The player is not standing outside this war. They are inside it.

---

## I. The Tension Matrix

### The Iron Weir
The Weir sees the frontier as a wound that must remain bound. Their aim is not conquest; it is endurance. They believe the old lines must keep standing until the world has time to stop shaking.

They are prepared to burn settlements, cut roads, and deny access to relic sites if they think the world will break wider.

### The Dawnward Choir
The Choir believes the future is not a matter of holding the line but of answering what lies beneath it. They see the frontier's instability as a promise: the Ancients are stirring and the world must be attuned.

They are not trying to win a war in the ordinary sense. They are trying to make the world legible to a force that is not human.

### The Dusk Ledger
The Dusk Ledger sees the frontier as a market of broken rights. They do not care whether a relic was meant for ritual, war, or memory. If it can be priced, it can be owned.

They weaponize scarcity. They shape the value of old knowledge by controlling access to it.

### Malachar's Vanguard
The Vanguard sees every ruin as a source of raw material. Their doctrine is old, brutal, and efficient: if a site has awakening residue, it should be extracted. If it resists, it should be broken.

They do not think like philosophers. They think like harvesters.

---

## II. The Fronts

### Front One — The Red Pass District
A chain of ruined watchpoints and pass-lights connected to the Old Guard's outer defenses. The Iron Weir maintains the route, but the Dawnward Choir believes the pass is a resonant artery and wants to use it for ritual alignment.

The Dusk Ledger wants access to the pass because the old warding works and sigils buried in its lower chambers can be sold as strategic relics.

### Front Two — The Lower Harbor Siege
The coastal ruin ports have become a staging ground for salvage operations and smuggling channels. Malachar's Vanguard floods these routes with pilots, labor crews, and extraction teams, while the Sable Harrow and the Tide Choir fight over who controls the actual waterline.

This front is not only military. It is economic and spiritual.

### Front Three — The Ash March
The broken road network separating the western frontier from the mountain holdings has become a battlefield of rotting supply depots, collapsed convoys, and forceful resettlement. The Weir uses it to keep people alive. Malachar uses it to strip the landscape of residue and recover old war material.

---

## III. The Cracks in the Map

The frontier war is not a war of conquest. It is a war of access.

Every contested location is defined by one of the following:
- a pass that can be opened or sealed
- a ruin that can be mined for old material
- a shrine that can become a resonant altar
- a route that determines whether a region survives a season
- a relic line that can be monetized or buried again

The deeper the war goes, the more its core logic becomes obvious: the frontier is no longer simply land. It is a wound in the world itself.

---

## IV. Key Commanders

### Marshal Veyra Dhal
Keeping the Weir disciplined while the frontier fractures. She is the last person who still believes the line can hold if the world is made to stop moving.

### Sister Halen Akor
The Choir's public voice and the one person capable of making a room feel as though it has been listening to itself for years. She sees the war as proof that the world is preparing to answer.

### Vesper Quill
The Dusk Ledger's chief negotiator of old things. He does not fight in the ordinary sense. He arranges the conditions under which other people fight, and then collects on the aftermath.

### Warden Serev Khar
Malachar's most competent front commander. He sees war as resource choreography and is almost never surprised by the cost of a campaign.

### Lady Ashrel Vein
The Threnody Houses have become more involved in the frontier war than any public statement admits. Her interest is not conquest; it is memory-control.

---

## V. The Player's Position

The player is the wildcard in this conflict.

They are not simply a soldier. They are a disruption engine. Every combat win, every relic recovered, every ruin crossed, every frontier wound touched is part of the same process: the world is being stirred toward a greater Awakening.

The player does not choose a side and stay there forever. The player crosses the world, discovers each faction's logic, and becomes the sort of variable that shifts the front itself.

---

## VI. What the War Leaves Behind

This conflict does not end with a single battlefield victory.

It leaves behind:
- broken roads that can never be repaired in simple ways
- relic roads and smuggling lines
- resonant dead sites that begin to glow at night
- people who are no longer sane enough to belong to a town or a faith
- old-guard structures that reveal they were never truly deactivated

The frontier cracks are not a decorative war arc. They are the visible structure of Aldenmor's larger crisis.

## VII. Expansion Hooks

1. A major route battle where the Iron Weir decides whether to blow the Red Pass entirely.
2. A hidden Dawnward Choir ritual chamber beneath a dead watchtower.
3. A Dusk Ledger auction where recovered old-guard artifacts are sold before their risks are understood.
4. A campaign where Malachar's Vanguard begins intentionally forcing collapsed ruins to awaken.
5. An old-guard survivor cell that wants to shut the entire frontier down rather than let it remain exploited.

This war arc is the bridge between local survival and world-scale upheaval.
